# Credit union network access readiness check (demo)

A clickable self-check landing page for credit union IT teams. Visitors answer 10 questions about how devices get onto their network and get a score out of 100, their biggest gaps, and the rule each gap traces back to.

Live page → https://taeyunkim03.github.io/Genians-Capstone/

## Why this idea

Our goal is to help the sponsor reach more small US credit unions with marketing we can actually test. A self-check fits for five reasons.

1. **It matches why credit unions buy NAC.** 58% of US credit unions have under $100M in assets, so most run IT with a tiny team, and all of them get examined. FFIEC exam guidance names NAC as a way to keep unauthorized devices off.
2. **It gives value before asking for anything.** The brand is little known in the US. A free result with no sign-up builds trust before we ask for an email.
3. **It's easy to share.** Credit unions find fintech partners mostly through peer referrals (79%) and trade groups (68%). The report can be copied and sent to a manager, the board or a peer.
4. **It stands out from the main competitor.** Portnox markets to credit unions mostly with webinars and case studies. An interactive tool is less common.
5. **It's measurable in a short test.** Visits, completions and emails left show up within 1 to 2 weeks, and the most common gaps tell us which message to lead with.

## How it works

- 8 scored questions in 4 areas. See what's connected, Control who gets on, Watch and keep records, Prove it.
- Each answer scores 2, 1 or 0. 80 and up is "Solid footing", 50 to 79 is "Some gaps", under 50 is "Big gaps".
- 2 unscored questions (switch brands, IT team size) tailor the "What to look for in a fix" advice.
- Every gap cites NCUA Part 748 Appendix A, FFIEC, NIST CSF 2.0 or CIS v8.1.
- Before anyone answers, the page shows a labeled example.

## Limits

- **Traffic is the hard part.** A page doesn't create awareness by itself. With an unknown brand, little or no budget and 1 to 2 weeks to test, we may only get a few dozen visitors, which is too few to conclude much.
- **It may overlap with ACET.** NCUA supports ACET, a free official cybersecurity self-assessment for credit unions. Our angle is that ours takes 3 minutes and focuses only on device access, but we need to check that users see the difference.
- **The person taking it may not be the buyer.** Many small credit unions outsource IT. If an outside IT provider makes the call, those providers may be the better channel. Portnox already co-hosts credit union webinars with one.
- **It doesn't use what already works.** The sponsor's credit union sales spread by referral. A real customer story probably beats any quiz.
